# Growth and evaluation plan

Virality and exponential growth cannot be guaranteed. RunProof's growth thesis is conditional: repeated model and hardware changes create recurring questions; useful passports lead to reproducible runs; contributors submit comparable receipts; those receipts make later passports more useful. Each arrow must be measured.

| Phase | Product milestone | Decision-quality KPI | Growth KPI | Stop or revise if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0: Offline reference | Estimator, importer, six-candidate synthetic passport | Zero false measured claims in fixtures; deterministic output | Number of independent developers who complete a local run | Users cannot understand estimate vs measurement |
| 1: Real hardware | Two model families, two engines and disclosed hardware runs | Reproducibility delta, SLO error, invalid-receipt rate | Run-to-submission conversion | Results vary so much that comparison is meaningless |
| 2: Public registry | Opt-in pages, exact-match search, portable badges | Replay agreement, stale-result detection, coverage by workload | Organic qualified visits, external links, repeat visits | Contributions require the core team to run every benchmark |
| 3: Operator integration | WorldOps shadow planning and private workload replay | P99 goodput, cost per SLO-compliant task, placement violations | Qualified pilot-to-paid conversion and retention | Optimization fails to improve matched baseline outcomes |

Recommended pre-registered pilot thresholds, **hypotheses rather than achieved results**: at least 90% of first-time testers understand the evidence labels in a comprehension task; at least 25% of qualified visitors run a relevant local test; at least 10% of completed tests yield a reusable, privacy-safe receipt; a measured result differs by no more than 15% on independent rerun under a frozen workload; and no hard-gate placement violation is accepted. Adjust thresholds with users before collecting results rather than after seeing them.

Unit economics: public cost per accepted receipt = runner compute + storage + review + abuse control + support divided by accepted receipts. Commercial gross margin = subscription/contract revenue minus customer-specific compute, storage, engineering support, sales and assurance costs. Buyer value must be measured as a difference in SLO-compliant task cost or prevented capacity purchase against a matched baseline; a modeled GPU-hour gap is not automatically cash savings.

Distribution channels to test: precise model × hardware pages, a command users can paste in a model card or GitHub issue, reproducible badges, upstream benchmark contributions, and opt-in comparison pages. Avoid publishing thousands of thin generated pages or claiming a universal score; evaluate search usefulness and evidence quality before scaling page count.
